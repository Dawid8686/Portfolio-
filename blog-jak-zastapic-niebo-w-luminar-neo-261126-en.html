Introduction to Sky Editing in Luminar Neo

Landscape photography can be unforgiving. You go out into the field, frame a beautiful view, and all nature gives you is a flat, white, or gray sky that strips the photo of all its dynamics. The traditional method of masking and cutting out such elements in graphic design software used to take hours and required advanced knowledge of curves, brushes, and layers. Fortunately, modern technology simplifies these tasks.

One of the most popular programs using artificial intelligence for photo editing is Luminar Neo. In this article, you will learn step by step how to replace sky in luminar neo so that the effect looks natural, without artificial halos around tree branches or buildings.

What is Sky AI and How Does It Work?

The Sky AI tool in Luminar Neo is an AI-driven algorithm that automatically detects the sky area in your photo, separating it from the foreground. The program analyzes edges, taking into account even complex elements such as tree crowns, masts, buildings, or human hair. Instead of manually painting a mask, you get a ready result in a fraction of a second.

However, it is worth remembering the program's limitations. Although the algorithm is advanced, it can struggle in photos with very low contrast, in dense fog, or when the horizon is completely blurred due to motion or poor focus. In such situations, delicate manual correction is necessary.

Step by Step: How to Replace Sky in Luminar Neo

The sky replacement process is very intuitive, but it requires a few specific actions to maintain the realism of the scene. Below is a step-by-step guide:

Step 1: Import and Prepare the Photo

Launch Luminar Neo and import the RAW or JPEG file you want to edit. Go to the 'Edit' tab in the upper right corner of the screen. It is recommended to perform basic exposure, contrast, and white balance corrections before changing the sky, because the new sky should match the lighting of the rest of the scene.

Step 2: Choose the Sky AI Tool

In the tool panel on the right side, find the 'Creative' section and click on the 'Sky AI' feature. After expanding the panel, you will see the main drop-down list with sky selection.

Step 3: Select the Appropriate Sky

Click the thumbnail of the current sky to open the library of available ready-made themes. You can choose dramatic clouds, a sunset, a blue sky with cirrus clouds, or upload your own graphic file (Custom Sky). When choosing a new sky, consider the direction of the light source in your photo – if the sun in the foreground shines from the right, choose a sky where the light source is in the same place.

Step 4: Adjust Position and Orientation

After selecting a new sky, the artificial intelligence will apply it immediately. Then use the adjustment sliders to perfectly fit it to the scene:

  • Horizon Vertical / Horizontal: Allow you to move the sky up or down and adjust the horizon line.
  • Blur: Useful when the focus of the foreground differs from the focus of the new sky.
  • Relight Scene: A crucial slider that adjusts the foreground colors to match the new sky, preventing the cutout effect.
  • Atmosphere Haze: Adds haze, which helps blend distant parts of the sky into the background.

Step 5: Masking and Edge Correction

If you notice that the algorithm missed a small fragment (e.g., a thin cable or a gap between leaves), use the masking brush in the 'Masking' section. You can use it to refine the edges, although in most cases the default algorithm is fully satisfactory.

How to Make Sky Replacement Look Natural?

Replacing the clouds is only half the battle. The most common mistake made by beginner photographers is failing to match the color temperature and exposure. If you insert dramatic, dark storm clouds into a landscape brightly lit by the sun, the photo will immediately betray digital editing.

Pay attention to:

  • Reflections in Water: If the photo features a lake, river, or wet surface, Luminar Neo has a sky reflection feature that must be properly activated.
  • Grain and Digital Noise: The new sky should not be perfectly smooth if the foreground is full of digital noise from the camera sensor.
  • Atmosphere and Time of Day: The golden hour requires warm tones in the sky and on terrestrial objects.
