Introduction to Artificial Background Blur

Do you dream of photos with a beautiful, softly blurred background and creamy highlights, but your lens has a narrow maximum aperture like f/4 or f/5.6? In the past, this meant investing in expensive portrait glass. Today, advanced artificial intelligence algorithms allow us to simulate shallow depth of field in post-processing. In this article, you will learn step by step how to create bokeh effect in Luminar Neo, even if your original photo was taken with a budget lens.

Luminar Neo offers dedicated tools for working with portraits. Although AI does a great job with plane detection, it rarely works one hundred percent perfectly without our input. Therefore, the key to success is combining automation with manual correction using the mask brush.

Preparation in the Edit Module

Before we start blurring, it is worth doing basic exposure correction. To make artificial bokeh look natural, the photo needs proper contrast and brightness. If the background contains small light sources, such as streetlamps, sun reflections in leaves, or small bulbs, the software will have something to build the light spots on.

  • Open Luminar Neo and import your chosen portrait photo.
  • Go to the Edit tab in the right-hand panel.
  • Perform basic white balance and correct exposure if the photo is too dark or blown out.

Portrait Background Removal Tool

The main tool responsible for separating the subject from the background is the Portrait Background Removal feature, located in the portrait tools section.

  • Find the Portrait section in the editing panel.
  • Select the Portrait Background Removal tool.
  • Slide the blur amount slider to the right. You will notice the background immediately loses sharpness while the subject remains sharp.

The algorithm analyzes the scene and creates a depth map. However, AI sometimes confuses hair, thin clothing elements, or spaces between hands and the torso with the background. Keep in mind the program's limitations: very complex backgrounds or subjects with low edge contrast may require manual intervention.

Adding the Bokeh Effect

Blurring alone is not enough to get a magical mood. Real bokeh is the aesthetic blur of light points in the out-of-focus zone. In the same tool, you will find sliders responsible for shaping bokeh lights.

  • Use the bokeh adjustment section that appears after activating background blur.
  • Increase the brightness or bokeh threshold to highlight bright spots in the background (e.g., light reflections).
  • Adjust the bokeh amount slider so you don't overdo the light spots – an excess of them can look unnatural.

Precise Correction with the Mask Brush

Since the automatic mask is rarely perfect, we need to refine it. The mask brush allows for manual addition or subtraction of areas from the depth map.

  • Turn on the mask view (often marked with a red or black-and-white preview icon) to see exactly what has been designated as background and what as the subject.
  • Select a brush with an appropriate size and edge softness.
  • Use the Brush: Paint option to paint over background areas that the algorithm mistakenly left sharp.
  • Use the Erase option if the blur mask spilled over onto the hair or clothing of the photographed person.

Carefully masking hair edges can take some time, but this exact step determines whether the photo looks professional or amateur. Spend a few minutes on this step.

Final Touches and Exporting

Once the background is properly blurred and the subject is sharp and naturally separated, it's worth returning to other correction tools in Luminar Neo to unify the whole composition.

  • Subtly increase contrast on the subject to visually draw the eye toward them.
  • Adjust vignetting to direct attention to the center of the frame.
  • If you want to test the full potential of the software, check out Luminar Neo and create your own bokeh effect on your photos.
  • Save the final file in JPEG or TIFF format at your chosen resolution.

By following these steps, even the simplest photo taken with a smartphone or a basic lens will gain depth and a cinematic character. Remember, however, that moderation is the key to success – excessive blur and overdone bokeh quickly give away digital tampering.
