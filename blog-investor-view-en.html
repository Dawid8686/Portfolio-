How an Investor Reads a Listing: Questions a Good Pitch Deck Answers

An investor browsing listings asks themselves the same questions before contacting a seller. A good pitch deck answers them in advance. Below are eight questions buyers typically ask, and ways to answer them honestly and clearly.

How a listing gets read

A buyer looks at many listings and searches for reasons to reject some and study others more closely. They typically check first what the business is, what it earns and how much work it needs, and only then look for risks. The deck's job is not to persuade at any cost, but to reduce the number of unknowns.

8 buyer questions

QuestionSlideHow to answer honestly
What is this business and who is it for?1-2One sentence and a specific customer.
What does it really earn?4-5Revenue and profit for a period you can document; explain spikes.
Where do customers come from?6Channels and dependence on a single source.
How much work does the owner need to do?8Hours per week and tasks that can be handed over.
What exactly am I buying?7List of assets, what is in the price and what is not.
Why are you selling?3A reason consistent with the rest of the listing.
What are the risks?6, 9Name them yourself: dependence on a platform, one client or one person.
What can I do with it next?10-11Realistic growth options and handover terms.

Slide numbers refer to the structure in the guide to a 12-slide pitch deck.

Trust matters more than decoration

  • Consistency. Numbers must match everywhere: in the listing, the deck and the documents. Discrepancies raise more doubts than a weaker result.
  • Openness about risks. A seller who names their own weak points looks more credible than one who lists none.
  • Easy verification. Keep the documents that support your numbers ready, so you can answer quickly when a buyer asks.

If you are selling internationally

  • Prepare the deck and listing in English, because most platforms and international buyers use it.
  • State amounts in one currency and label it clearly. If you convert, give the rate and date.
  • Say when and how you can be contacted.

Checklist before sending

  1. Does every slide answer one of the eight questions?
  2. Is every number backed by documents?
  3. Is the reason for sale honest and consistent with the rest of the listing?
  4. Have you named the main risks?
  5. Do the photos and screenshots show the real state of things? (see the guide on photos)
