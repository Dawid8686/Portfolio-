7 Mistakes in Business Listing Photos and How to Fix Them

A buyer judges a listing with their eyes first and the numbers second. Amateur photos do not make a good business bad, but they can weaken it before anyone reads the results. Here are seven common mistakes and how to fix them without misleading anyone.

Why photos matter

Photos are a buyer's first contact with the business. They cannot replace financial results, but they influence whether the listing is taken seriously and whether the buyer will check the rest. Good photos show the business as it is, in the best possible light.

7 mistakes and how to fix them

  1. Dark or uneven light. Photos taken under mixed lighting have different shades of white. Shoot in daylight and use an editor to even out exposure and white balance.
  2. Clutter in the frame. Cables, boxes and personal items distract. Tidy up physically first. In the editor, remove only small, temporary items, never permanent flaws.
  3. Inconsistent style. Photos with different colours and backgrounds look like they come from several businesses. Choose one tone (colour, contrast) and apply it to the whole series.
  4. Low technical quality. Blur, grain and low resolution suggest carelessness. Shoot from a support or tripod at the highest quality and do not over-sharpen.
  5. Unreadable analytics screenshots. Take a clear screenshot of the whole view with the date range, and cover customers' personal data with simple blur or a box. You may cover personal data, never the performance figures.
  6. Stock photos pretending to be the real business. A stock image of a "team" or "office" is misleading. Use stock only as mood illustration and with a valid licence.
  7. Over-retouching. Smoothed, artificial photos breed distrust, and the buyer will see the business live or on a video call anyway. The goal is a faithful but tidy picture.

Where honest retouching ends

Retouch the look, not the facts. Improve light, colour and tidiness in your photos. Do not hide defects, damage or gaps, do not add equipment the business does not have, and never change the numbers in analytics screenshots. Buyers check these during due diligence, and misleading them can end the deal.

A simple step-by-step process

  1. Tidy up and shoot in daylight.
  2. Pick the best shots, a few per area instead of many similar ones.
  3. Even out light and colour in an editor such as Luminar Neo (a photo editor with AI tools; available features depend on the plan, check the current offer).
  4. Remove small, temporary items that are not part of the business.
  5. Save the whole series in one style and the same resolution.
  6. Before publishing, check that every photo shows the real state of things.
