How AI Technology Works in Luminar Neo

Before moving on to the practice, it is worth understanding the mechanism behind the software. The AI engine analyzes the environment of the removed object—texture, lighting, shadows, and perspective. Then, it generates missing pixels based on samples from adjacent areas. This means that the more repetitive or smooth the background is (e.g., sky, sand, asphalt, grass), the better and more realistic the final result will be.

Step-by-Step: How to Remove Unwanted Objects in Luminar Neo

The entire process is designed to be intuitive for both amateurs and professionals. Here is a step-by-step instruction on how to complete this task:

  • Step 1: Launch Luminar Neo and import your chosen photo into the catalog or open it in the Single Image Edit module.
  • Step 2: Go to the Edit tab in the right sidebar. Find the Essentials section and select the Erase tool.
  • Step 3: Adjust the Brush Size using the slider or keyboard shortcuts (square brackets [ and ]) to precisely cover the object you want to remove.
  • Step 4: Brush over the entire unwanted element. You don't need to be surgically precise, but try not to overlap too much onto the background that should remain untouched. For people or power lines, the program also offers dedicated automatic tools described below.
  • Step 5: Click the Erase button located under the brush panel. The algorithm will analyze the selection and fill the resulting empty space.
  • Step 6: Evaluate the result. If the edges look unnatural or the program leaves a trace, use the Clone & Stamp tool or repeat the operation with a smaller brush on the problematic area.

Automatic Removal of People and Power Lines

Luminar Neo features built-in automated functions that significantly speed up work on standard compositional problems:

  • Erase Power Lines: If wires or cables appear in your urban landscape or mountain view, just click one command. The algorithm independently detects thin, linear elements and removes them in a fraction of a second.
  • Erase Dust Spots: Useful for sensor dust spots that appear when the aperture is stopped down.

Honest Assessment of Program Limitations

No AI tool is perfect, and it is worth knowing the limitations of Luminar Neo to avoid frustration:

  • Complex Backgrounds: If you remove a figure walking past a complex pattern (e.g., a crowd of people, complicated architecture with many details), the AI may generate visible texture repetitions or blurring. In such cases, manual correction is often necessary.
  • Shadows and Reflections: When remembering to remove the main object (e.g., a car), it is easy to forget its shadow cast on the ground or reflection in a window or water. AI does not always automatically remove associated shadows, so they must be painted over separately.
  • High Resolution: For very heavy files (e.g., high-megapixel RAW panoramas), the rendering process may take a moment and requires adequate computer hardware resources.

Tips for Best Results

To minimize the need for manual corrections, it is worth following a few proven rules when working with the erase tool:

  • Work at 100% zoom. This allows you to spot micro-errors in the pixel structure before saving the final file.
  • Break large objects into smaller parts. Instead of painting over the entire figure in one huge stroke, remove it in fragments (e.g., torso first, then legs). This gives artificial intelligence better reference points from the environment.
  • Use editing history. If the AI result ruins the frame, you can always step back (Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z) and try selecting the object slightly differently.
