How to Build a Business-for-Sale Pitch Deck (12 Slides, Canva Template)

Also available in PL NL

A buyer decides in the first 30 seconds whether your business looks serious. The numbers matter, but the way they are presented decides whether anyone reads them. Below is a 12-slide structure you can build in an evening, plus the design rules that make it look professional.

What a buyer wants to see in 30 seconds

A buyer scanning listings wants three answers fast: what the business does, how much it earns and how much work it takes. Everything else supports those three. A clean, consistent deck signals that the books and operations are probably just as tidy; a messy one raises doubts before the first call.

The 12 slides

#SlideWhat goes in
1TitleLogo, business name, one-line pitch, asking price or range.
2SnapshotWhat it does, for whom, since when, where it is based.
3Reason for saleOne honest sentence. Buyers always ask.
4Key numbersRevenue and profit for 12-24 months as a simple chart.
5Revenue modelWhere money comes from, split by source (%).
6Customers and trafficChannels, repeat rate, top sources. Screenshots of analytics.
7Assets includedDomain, website, social accounts, content, customer list, IP, supplier contracts.
8Owner workloadHours per week the owner really spends, and on what.
9Team and toolsWho does what, software used, documented processes.
10Growth potential2-3 concrete things a new owner could do next.
11Deal termsPrice, what is included, handover and support period.
12Next stepContact details, NDA, how to request full financials.

Use only figures you can back up with documents. Buyers verify them during due diligence.

Build it in Canva in 5 steps

  1. Start from a presentation template in Canva and choose a layout with plenty of white space.
  2. Set your Brand Kit (logo, 2-3 colours, 2 fonts). With Canva Pro you can store it and apply it to every slide in one click. Check the current plans on Canva's site.
  3. Create 12 pages, one per slide from the table above. One idea per slide.
  4. Turn numbers into charts using Canva's chart elements instead of pasting spreadsheets.
  5. Export as PDF for sending, and keep the editable version for updates.

Where the visuals come from

  • Real screenshots of your store, analytics and dashboards are your strongest evidence. Blur customer data.
  • Adobe Stock for backgrounds, textures, icons and mood images on title and section slides. Buy the licence for each file you use (not the watermarked preview) and check that the licence covers your use. You can also browse my Adobe Stock portfolio.
  • Photoshop or Lightroom to clean up product photos: consistent light, background and colour across all items.
  • Illustrator if your logo needs redrawing as a clean vector file.

Never use stock photos as your "team", "customers" or "premises". Stock is for atmosphere only. Presenting it as fact misleads buyers and can end a deal.

Design rules that make it look professional

  • Two fonts maximum, three colours maximum, the same layout grid on every slide.
  • Large numbers, short labels. If a slide needs more than 40 words, split it.
  • High contrast between text and background, especially over photos.
  • Same style for all charts and screenshots (rounded corners, shadows or none, not mixed).
  • Read it on a phone: many buyers will open the PDF there first.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding the reason for sale or the owner's real workload.
  • Round, unexplained numbers with no source.
  • Walls of text instead of one clear message per slide.
  • Mixing several visual styles from different template packs.
  • No clear next step on the last slide.
