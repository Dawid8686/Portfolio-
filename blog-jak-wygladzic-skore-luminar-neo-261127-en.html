Introduction to natural portrait retouching

Portrait retouching is often associated with excessive smoothing that turns skin into a plastic surface devoid of pores and natural features. In digital photography, the key to success is balance – removing imperfections while preserving the unique skin texture. Artificial intelligence built into modern photo editing software comes to the rescue. If you are wondering how to smooth skin in Luminar Neo in a way that remains invisible to the viewer, this guide will walk you through the entire process step by step.

Luminar Neo offers a dedicated tool called Skin AI, which automatically detects a face in a photo and separates the skin from clothing, background, and hair. This makes the editing precise and eliminates the need for time-consuming brush masking in most standard cases.

Preparing your image for editing in Luminar Neo

Before moving on to skin retouching itself, it is worth performing basic exposure and contrast correction. A well-lit photo helps AI algorithms correctly detect facial features and texture.

    Launch Luminar Neo and import your RAW or JPEG file.
    Go to the Develop tab to correct white balance, blown highlights, and facial shadows.
    Pay attention to contrast – high contrast can emphasize fine wrinkles and pores, making later smoothing more difficult.
    Once the photo is prepared, move to the Edit section, where portrait tools are located.

The Skin AI tool step by step

The main element responsible for analyzing and modifying the complexion is the Skin AI module. You will find it in the right-hand panel under the Portrait section. This is where the answer to how to smooth skin in Luminar Neo lies, without using complicated Frequency Separation techniques.

    Expand the Skin AI tab and locate the Amount slider.
    Move the slider to the right while observing changes on the model's face. The algorithm will automatically smooth uneven skin tone and minor blemishes.
    Learn more about the program capabilities here: check out Luminar Neo to explore the full suite of AI-powered features.
    Avoid setting the slider to 100% unless you are aiming for a CGI 3D graphic effect. Values between 30% and 50% are usually enough to achieve a clean yet natural look.

Preserving skin texture with Shine Removal

The most common mistake during skin smoothing is completely removing natural pores. To prevent this, Luminar Neo provides additional control sliders within the same module.

Pay attention to the Shine Removal feature. Facial skin often reflects flash or sunlight, creating unsightly oily patches and making the complexion look greasy. This slider reduces excessive shine without destroying the skin structure underneath.

    Use the Shine Removal slider to mattify the T-zone (forehead, nose, chin).
    Watch how skin pores remain intact while the face loses only the unnecessary, flat glare.
    If the portrait features a child or an elderly person, exercise extreme caution – overly aggressive removal of wrinkles or natural facial features will strip the photo of realism.

Advanced masking and local correction

AI algorithms are extremely advanced, but artificial intelligence can sometimes mistake skin texture for background elements of similar tone or unnecessarily affect the lips and eyes. In such situations, manual control is essential.

Luminar Neo allows for quick correction of the mask where Skin AI operates:

    Click the brush icon next to the Skin AI module to preview or edit the generated mask.
    Use the Eraser brush to remove the effect from the eyes, eyebrows, lips, and hairline.
    If necessary, you can also manually paint over skin areas that the algorithm failed to detect automatically.

An honest look at software limitations

As a professional AI-based tool, Luminar Neo significantly speeds up work, but it has its limitations. Algorithms handle standard portraits very well, but under specific conditions, they may require human support.

First of all, Skin AI may struggle with very low-resolution images or difficult lighting conditions where shadows and digital noise overlap with skin texture. In such cases, artificial intelligence may interpret noise as skin blemishes and blur it unnaturally. Therefore, it is always recommended to denoise the RAW file beforehand.

Another limitation is the lack of full control over stray hairs falling onto the face or specific scars that the program might try to remove as standard pimples. In these situations, using the traditional Clone & Stamp or Erase tool is necessary.

Conclusion

Portrait retouching does not have to be difficult or time-consuming. Knowing how to smooth skin in Luminar Neo using Skin AI saves hours of work spent on manually stamping out every imperfection. However, remember that moderation is the key to good photography. Always prioritize preserving natural texture, control effect intensity with sliders, and manually refine masks when needed. This approach will give your portraits a professional yet authentic character.
